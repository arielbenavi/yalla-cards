## What was learned — read this before touching content

### chatifai invents things. Four caught this session.

| fabrication | where | what it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| `לַוְזִין` / `לַקְטַה` | market dialogue | offered as "premium produce"; neither corroborated |
| `עַ-בַּלַאש` | cafe dialogue | glossed "meanwhile"; means **for free** |
| `מוחד` / `موخد` | taxi dialogue | not a word at all, in both columns |
| `מַיַّאתִי` | possessive verification | not a real inflection of مي |

**Never pass a chatifai replacement through without corroborating it.** When it
cannot be corroborated, prefer deleting the phrase over substituting — a shorter
correct sentence beats a longer invented one. This is now standing practice and
it has held up four times.

### chatifai's "polished version" silently corrupts

It routinely marks a line תקין and then rewrites it in its summary block,
dropping shaddas throughout. The worst instance: it wrote `וַקֵע` for `וַקֵّע` on
a line it had just called "תקין מאוד" — that shadda is the whole difference
between وقّع (sign) and وقع (fall), so its improvement turned "sign here" into
"fall here".

**Take the per-line verdicts. Never paste the polished block.**

Its summary counts are also unreliable — it reported ض in lines that contain no
ض three separate times, while its individual line entries were correct.

### The stored dialogues are not chatifai content

The 15 `simulation_*` rows in `paradigms` were generated by a general-purpose
model. Evidence: ض transliterated as ד׳ in **four audited dialogues out of four**,
and `شقل` for shekel appearing in zero cards but four dialogues. Both are now
fixed mechanically across all 15 by `scripts/apply-dialogue-fixes.ts`, which
applies global word corrections to every dialogue and per-line audited fixes only
to those chatifai has reviewed. `scripts/scan-dialogue-dad-errors.ts` proves zero
ض errors remain.

**The mechanical pass does not mark anything verified.** Fixing a known
transcription defect is not the same as having content reviewed.

### The ق convention — ruled, not applied

**How it was unstuck.** For weeks chatifai gave a rule, then contradicted it with
its own examples. What worked was to stop asking *what the rule is* and ask for a
verdict on **every word containing ק that actually exists in the cards** —
`scripts/list-qaf-words.ts` produces that list, and ק only ever transliterates ق
here (ك is כּ), so the list is the exact scope. Given 83 concrete words in four
batches it ruled all 83 with no skips and no "not sure". Generalise the technique:
*ask this source for judgements on real instances, not for rules.*

`scripts/data/qaf-ruling.ts` holds every verdict verbatim. The rule: ق → א
everywhere **except** `קַבֵּל`/`קַבְּל`, `רַקַם`, `קוֹלַה` (brand), `תַוְפִיק`
(proper name), and the colours `זַרְקַא`, `זֻרֹק`, `שַׁקְרַא`, `קַמְחִי`. A dry
run confirms coverage is total: the only unmatched ק words in the whole database
are those nine exceptions.

**It also changes more than ق.** Vowels shift with the consonant
(`קֵצֵّה→אֻצַّה`, `אֵ→אִ`, `מִ→מַ`), plus `כ→כּ` and final `צ→ץ`. Asked directly
whether to apply only the ق swap or the whole form — and told these are cards
Ariel has already memorised — it said apply the whole form: converting only the ק
would leave `אִצַّה`, which no urban speaker says.

**Still not applied**, for two reasons recorded in `BLOCKERS`: `דַקִיקַה` got two
different forms in two rounds (`דַאִיאַה` vs `דַאִיה`) and is held out; and the
conversion rewrites `translit_nikud` on cards with existing FSRS history — 76
cards change, **24 of them have real review history** — which is Ariel's call,
not the agent's.

`scripts/apply-qaf-ruling.ts` is ready: dry run by default, full JSON backup
before writing, and `--revert <backup.json>` to undo. Nothing else on this project
rewrites memorised text, so nothing else needs that shape — but this does.

Watch for chatifai referring to this as settled — it wrote "כפי שסיכמנו" about it
while it was still parked.

**The dialogues are already inconsistent about it**: `הַלְאֵית` in car_trouble and
family_chat, `הַלְקֵית` in clothes_shop; `אַדֵّיש` against `קַדֵّיש`. Also `וִאְפַת`,
`תִאְלַא`, `אִסְתִאְבַּאל`, `מִנְאֻעֻד`, `סוּא`. The cards are uniformly ק, so the
dialogues are the outliers — but normalising them is still the same one
decision and must not be done piecemeal.

**chatifai contradicts itself on ظ.** In the doctor audit it ruled ז׳ wrong and
ט׳ right, with reasoning. In the self_intro audit it marked ז׳ "correct" with
none. The cards write ט׳ and that stands. A reasoned ruling outweighs a bare
approval — and this is the second convention it has been inconsistent about.

### Data hazards that bit this session

- **Card inserts and `card_srs` inserts are separate statements.** 199 of 820
  cards had no `card_srs` row and could never appear in review, including all 82
  of מפגש בעפ 2. Same gap exists for `verb_conjugations` → `conjugation_srs`.
  Always create the SRS row and verify.
- **Seed migrations without `ON CONFLICT` get re-applied.** `0013_seed_verbs.sql`
  ran seven times. `scripts/migrate.ts` now tracks applied migrations.
- **Arabic marks hide inside Hebrew transliteration.** Arabic shadda U+0651 and
  Hebrew dagesh U+05BC render identically. 61 cards had the Arabic one. This is
  not cosmetic: `translit_normalized` strips `[֑-ׇ]`, which is Hebrew marks
  only, so U+0651 survives normalisation and dedup can never pair those cards.
  `scripts/scan-script-mixing.ts` checks cards, verbs, possessives, songs and
  dialogues. Easy to reintroduce by typing — I did it twice in one session.
  **Pasting chatifai's output verbatim reintroduces it too**, because its Hebrew
  transliteration routinely carries the Arabic mark. `scripts/lib/normalize-marks.ts`
  is the guard: `normalizeMarks()` maps the five Arabic marks to their Hebrew
  equivalents *only in text whose letters are Hebrew*, and `assertClean()` throws
  on anything still mixed. Every insert path must call both **before** validating.
  This is a codepoint correction, not an edit to what chatifai said — the same
  mark, the right codepoint — and it is the only adjustment permitted to its output.
- **`.in()` over ~850 ids overflows the PostgREST URL** and returns empty
  silently. Fetch small tables whole and join in memory.
- **A plain `select()` caps at 1000 rows.** `review_log` is well past that, so an
  unpaged read undercounts reviews — it reported 0 for a card with 2 and nearly
  made the duplicate merge keep the wrong side. Page every read of a big table.
- **A test that asserts a proxy fails on success.** `dialogue-tab.spec.ts`
  asserted `all.total > served`, which only held while some dialogue was still
  unverified — verifying the fifteenth turned it red. Assert the property you
  mean, not a side effect that happens to correlate with it. Note also that the
  pre-push hook runs `next build`, **not** Playwright, so a red e2e test will
  push. Run `npx playwright test` yourself before pushing.
- **`LIKE` against a uuid column errors** ("operator does not exist: uuid ~~
  unknown"). Fetch and prefix-match in JS.
- **Nikud normalisation differs** between Postgres and a TS source literal.
  Compare with `.normalize("NFC")` or identical-looking strings won't match.
- **`npx tsc --noEmit` is weaker than `npx next build`.** A Supabase upsert over
  differently-shaped array elements passes one and fails the other. Build before
  pushing; a pre-push hook runs it anyway.
- **Playwright reuses a running dev server.** Restart it after changing an API
  route or the tests run against stale code.

### The chatifai agent has no Bash tool

Only Chrome tools. Never send it a script to run or ask it to query the DB — dump
the rows yourself and paste the text into the message. Ask about transliteration
as an explicit numbered question; left implicit, chatifai answers only about
naturalness and silently alters vocalisation.

### 5. Song repair (`efbd5595`) — read the index hazard first

**`song_word_srs.word_index` is a positional index into the flattened
`lyrics_parsed[].words`.** `app/api/songs/[id]/review/route.ts:77` resolves a
review row with `allWords[row.word_index]`. Inserting a missing word anywhere but
the very end therefore **shifts every later index and silently re-points existing
review history at the wrong word**. 150 `song_word_srs` rows exist today, 79 of
them on Tisma'ani — which is also one of the songs with missing glosses. So the
obvious repair is the one that corrupts data.

Two safe routes, and this is a design decision, not a cleanup:
1. Append missing words at the end of the last line's `words` array. Indices only
   grow, existing rows stay valid. Cheap, but the review order stops matching the
   song.
2. Key `song_word_srs` on the word text instead of its position, via a migration.
   Correct, and it removes the hazard permanently.

**Two measurement errors here, and both are worth knowing about.**

*First:* a gloss entry can cover several words at once — `يا عمري` is stored as
one entry meaning "חיי שלי", which is *better* than glossing يا and عمري apart,
because the phrase is not the sum of them. Comparing entry-for-word reported all
of those as missing and produced a count of 37 across six songs. The real number
is under 10, and Tisma'ani, דבקה בתל אביב and Allah Aleik Ya Seedy were clean all
along.

*Second, and this one cost a revert:* even the corrected count is **candidates,
not defects**. The lyrics use spelling variants freely, and no string comparison
can see through them:

| reported missing | already glossed as |
|---|---|
| `كله` | `كلو` |
| `مهدومة` | `محضومة` |
| `واللي` | covered by `اللي جاي` / `اللي غاب` |
| `وأنا` | `و` + `أنا` as two entries |

`scripts/fill-song-glosses.ts` inserted five words into YAMA; four were wrong —
three duplicates and one placed in the wrong line — and both ZIDI inserts
duplicated existing glosses. All of it was reverted with
`scripts/revert-song-glosses.ts` and verified back to byte-identical: 122 and 147
words, all 71 YAMA review rows on their original indices.

**Before filling any reported gap, check the line for the same word under another
spelling.** That reconciliation is an Arabic judgement and belongs with chatifai,
not with a normaliser.

**The index machinery itself is sound and worth keeping.** `lib/song-words.ts`
computes the moves, refuses when anything does not line up, and writes
highest-target-first so a row never lands on a position still held by a row yet
to move — 9 unit tests. The verification after the (wrong) write showed all 71
YAMA rows still resolving to the right words, so the remap did its job even
though the content decision above it was bad. Every run backs up to
`backups/song-<id>-<ts>.json` first, which is the only reason the revert was
possible.

### 7. The inflections recognition track was testing the wrong thing
Fixed. It printed the pronoun and asked which verb the form meant, so the person
was given away in the prompt and the inflection could be ignored — that is what
`5e4b20e5` meant by "נטיות לא טוב". It now shows the form with its meaning and
asks **which person**, with distractors drawn from the same verb (a different
verb is distinguishable by its stem alone, which is the same shortcut again).

The rule that needed a test: Palestinian present tense really does spell inta and
hiyye alike, so a naive person-picker offers both and marks one wrong. Any person
sharing the target's spelling is excluded from the options and accepted when
chosen. `lib/inflections.ts`, 10 unit tests.
